Ticket Court

Type your plate. A team of AI lawyers pulls every ticket you have, checks each one against the actual street signs, and files the ones you can win.

Why it's real: NYC publishes every parking and camera ticket, about 36 million records, with plate, time, violation, fine, a link to the ticket image and the hearing outcome, including "NOT GUILTY." It also publishes every DOT parking sign by block. So an agent can check the city's own sign data against the city's own ticket and find the ones that don't match.

The agent team

Clerk Open Data

Pulls every ticket for the plate from the Parking Violations dataset. One Daytona sandbox per ticket.

Investigators ×N Daytona

One per ticket, running in parallel. Each runs Python on that block's DOT sign rules, the violation time and past hearing outcomes for that violation type and precinct, then scores the win chance.

Case file SurrealDB

Shared memory across all agents. "Open" is replaced by "disputed," then by "dismissed," with history kept. It remembers your car, your usual blocks, and past wins.

Filer Anchor

Opens the city's Department of Finance dispute page in a cloud browser, fills it with the evidence, and stops before Submit so you approve. Live view on the big screen.

Caller ElevenLabs

"You got a ticket on Liberty Ave at 9:14. The sign there allows parking until 10. Want me to fight it?" Say yes, and it files.

Auditor Arize

Traces every agent and grades every claim. Each argument must cite a real sign or ticket record, or it gets dropped.

Unclaimed

Call one number and speak any language. An AI caseworker finds every benefit your household qualifies for, then fills in the applications while you watch.

Why it's real: NYC publishes the city's Benefits and Programs dataset (40+ federal, state and city programs, in several languages) and the ACCESS NYC Screening API, the actual eligibility rules behind the city's screener. Most people never finish the screener: it's long, in English-first government language, and online only.

The agent team

Voice intake ElevenLabs

A warm phone-style conversation in the caller's language: household size, income, rent, kids, age. One question at a time.

Household memory SurrealDB

Facts with dates. "Lost job in September" replaces the old income, and history is kept. Shared by every agent so nobody asks twice.

Screener Open Data

Runs the household against the city's eligibility rules and program dataset, ranks programs by dollars per year, and explains each in plain words.

Document vault Daytona

Pay stubs and leases get read inside a throwaway sandbox that deletes itself afterward. Sensitive papers never sit on a server.

Form filler Anchor

Opens the real program page in a cloud browser and fills it from memory, on screen. Stops before Submit for the person to confirm.

Safety check Arize

Every eligibility claim must trace back to a rule. If the agent isn't sure, it says "check with a caseworker" instead of guessing.

2:25

Money. Free for residents. Nonprofit plan per caseworker seat: a dashboard of all clients, deadline reminders and renewals. RevenueCat handles the subscription.

Signal Room

A swarm of AI investigators watches every 311 call in the city, spots the building or block that's quietly falling apart, digs up who's responsible, and briefs the people who can fix it.

Why it's real: crises hide in plain sight in 311. One Brooklyn building filed 83 heat complaints in 18 days this month, but the data is split across 311, HPD violations (38,609 inspections since Oct 1) and building records. Nobody connects it until a reporter does, weeks later.

The agent team

Watcher Open Data

Pulls fresh 311 complaints and flags spikes: same building, same block, same complaint type, rising fast.

Investigators ×N Daytona

Each hotspot gets its own sandbox agent that runs Python: HPD violations, complaint history and the trend against neighbors. They run in parallel, and judges watch them spawn.

Records runner Anchor

Browses public city record sites that have no API (building and owner lookups) to find who is responsible.

City memory SurrealDB

Shared, time-aware knowledge: "heat out since Oct 3" is later replaced by "restored Oct 15." Each hotspot keeps its full story and sources.

Editor Arize

Every finding must trace to a record. Unsupported claims get cut before they reach a briefing. The dashboard proves it.

Briefing ElevenLabs

A 60-second morning audio briefing for a council member or reporter: "Three buildings in your district need attention today…"

Six more options

Same full-stack approach, shorter write-ups. Each one names the city data it uses (all checked live on Oct 19) and what each sponsor tool does in it.

Mock Inspector B2B · $29/mo

A restaurant owner films the kitchen. Vision agents (one Daytona sandbox per camera angle) check the footage against the health code violations restaurants actually get cited for, and give a practice grade. Anchor pulls the restaurant's own inspection record, SurrealDB tracks fixes until the real inspection, and an ElevenLabs voice coach talks the staff through the fixes. Wow: film the venue's kitchen live. Data: Restaurant Inspections.

Violation Zero Landlords · $19/bldg

The flip side of tenant apps. Small landlords (1–10 units) get an AI property manager: daily alerts for new HPD violations and 311 complaints, a plain-English fix and deadline for each, and a voice call to the super. Anchor finds licensed contractors, and memory tracks each violation from open to fixed to certified. Why it wins: landlords pay, so the business is obvious. Data: HPD Violations, 311.

Eviction Shield Legal aid orgs

A tenant photographs their court papers. A self-deleting Daytona sandbox reads them and pulls out the deadlines. An ElevenLabs voice explains them in the tenant's language and points to NYC's free-lawyer program (Right to Counsel). Anchor looks up the case on the public court site, and memory sends reminders before each court date. Data: 14,477 evictions carried out in NYC so far in 2026, 4,616 of them in the Bronx.

After Hours Watch Residents + boards

Drilling at 11pm? Snap a photo or record 10 seconds of audio. The agent checks whether the site has an after-hours permit from the Department of Buildings. No permit means it builds a timestamped evidence report and files it. Multiple agents track each building site over weeks, and memory notes "permit expired Oct 12." Data: DOB After Hour Variance Permits, DOB Permit Issuance, 311 construction noise.

Permit Pilot Small business

"I want to open a café in Astoria." A swarm of agents works out every permit and license needed, in order. Anchor checks the city business sites that have no API, memory tracks each application's status, and voice check-ins say what's next. Wow: a live checklist building itself as sandboxes finish. Data: DOB Permit Issuance, plus city business licensing pages.

Block Stories Consumer · $4.99/mo

Point your phone at any building and an ElevenLabs voice guide tells its story: what was built and when, renovations, and which TV shows filmed on the block. SurrealDB remembers your walks so tours never repeat, and parallel agents research the next blocks ahead of you. Why RevenueCat loves it: a consumer subscription app is exactly their core customer. Data: Film Permits, DOB Permits.
