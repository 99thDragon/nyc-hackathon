Noise Pulse

Your phone measures the noise. The city's complaints back you up. AI turns both into evidence that gets acted on.

The problem, in the city's own numbers

32,788apartment noise complaints to 311 in the last 30 days
12,300of those closed as "no evidence of violation." The noise had stopped by the time police came
67,000+noise complaints of all kinds in 30 days

The insight: people do complain, but by the time someone shows up there's no proof. Noise Pulse captures the proof while it happens and groups neighbors so one strong report replaces 40 that get ignored.

How it works

SenseScan a QR code. The phone mic measures loudness. No app to install.
MapYour reading pins to your block, next to that block's 311 noise history.
DecideAI spots the pattern: time, day, source type, how many neighbors.
ActDrafts one combined, evidence-backed complaint for the block.
Follow upNext night, it checks whether readings dropped.

3-minute demo script

0:00

Hook. "Raise your hand if a neighbor's noise kept you up this year." (Hands go up.) "Last month 12,300 noise complaints closed as no evidence. The noise was real. The proof wasn't."

0:30

Audience moment. QR code on screen. "Scan this. On three, everybody make noise." The big screen's map pulses and readings stack up live.

1:15

Zoom out. Switch to a real block. The map shows that block's 311 noise history plus the night's readings.

1:45

AI moment. Tap "Build report." AI writes: "Fri and Sat 11pm–2am, average 82, 14 neighbors, 31 past complaints, likely commercial source." Ready for 311 or the community board.

2:20

Business. Free for neighbors. Pro $4.99/mo: a noise report for any address before you sign a lease, plus alerts. Paywall powered by RevenueCat.

2:45

Close. "Proof, not complaints." Thank the judges.

What could break

Phones give relative loudness, not true decibels.Browsers can't calibrate the mic, and iPhones ignore the "turn off auto-volume" setting. Say "loudness score" on screen, never "dB," unless a judge asks.
The mic only works on a secure link.It must be the Vercel https link. Opening it from a laptop's local address on a phone will fail.
Venue Wi-Fi with 40 phones.Do the audience moment with 5–10 phones, and have a teammate's phone already connected as backup.
Live updates across phones.This is the hardest part. If it fights you past the halfway mark, have the wall refresh every 2 seconds instead. It looks the same on stage.

Snap & Solve

Take a photo of what's broken. AI tells you who fixes it, whether neighbors already reported it, and keeps pushing until it's fixed.

The problem, in the city's own numbers

37%of streetlight reports from July 2026 are still not closed three months later (1,183 of 3,212)
7,213street-condition reports (potholes and similar) in 30 days
~200311 complaint types. Most people don't know which one to pick

The insight: reporting is confusing, duplicates pile up, and nobody follows up. Snap & Solve fixes all three: it picks the category for you, merges you with neighbors who already reported, and keeps pushing after the report is filed. That last step is the Relay idea.

How it works

SenseSnap a photo. The phone camera opens straight from the web page.
DecideClaude looks at the photo: what it is, how serious, which agency and 311 category.
CheckSearches 311 within 150 m for the same issue. Already reported? Joins that report instead.
ActWrites the report, ready to send, with photo and location.
Follow upStill open after X days? Drafts an escalation to the council member with the full history.

0:00

Hook. Photo of a dark street. "This streetlight was reported in July. It's still out. Over a third of July's streetlight reports are."

0:30

Live snap. Hand a judge your phone and a printed photo of a pothole. They snap it.

0:45

AI moment. About 5 seconds later: "Pothole, high severity, DOT, category Street Condition." Then: "3 neighbors already reported this since Sept 2. Join them?"

1:30

Follow-up. Jump to a report that's 30 days old. It shows "Still open," with a drafted escalation to the council member listing every date.

2:15

Business. Free for residents. Pro for building managers and business districts ($19/mo): track every issue on your blocks in one dashboard. RevenueCat paywall.

2:45

Close. "Don't just report it. Get it fixed."

Heat Line

One message starts it. The agent organizes the building, builds the evidence, and checks back until the heat is on.

The problem, in the city's own numbers

8,106no-heat / no-hot-water complaints in the first 18 days of heat season (Oct 1–18)
83complaints from a single Brooklyn building in those 18 days
341,824heat complaints last season (Oct 2025–May 2026)

The insight: tenants in the same building complain one by one, without evidence, and give up. The law is clear: 68°F inside from 6am–10pm when it's below 55°F outside, and 62°F overnight all season. Heat Line turns scattered complaints into a building-wide case that doesn't close until a tenant says the heat is back.

How it works

SenseA tenant messages "no heat," plus the indoor temperature if they have it.
DecideAgent looks up the building's heat history and today's outdoor temperature. Is this a legal violation right now?
OrganizeAsks neighbors who signed up: "Heat out for you too?"
ActBuilds a timeline and drafts the HPD complaint and a landlord letter.
Follow upEvery 12 hours: "Heat back yet?" Stays open until a tenant confirms.

3-minute demo script

0:00

Hook. "Heat season started 18 days ago. One building in Brooklyn has already filed 83 complaints. 83. Still cold."

0:30

Live chat. Judge scans a QR code and types "no heat, it's 61 inside" as a tenant of a real building.

0:50

AI moment. Agent replies: "It's 49°F outside, so your landlord must keep it at 68°F. That's a violation. Your building had 12 heat complaints this month. I've asked your 4 signed-up neighbors."

1:30

Building view. Big screen: neighbor replies come in, a timeline builds, and the HPD complaint is drafted with every date.

2:10

The loop. Fast-forward: "12 hours later, heat back yet?" The case stays open until a tenant confirms. "Relief isn't delivered until the tenant says so."

2:30

Business. Free for tenants. Tenant associations $9/mo: building-wide dashboard plus evidence packets for housing court. RevenueCat paywall.

1 · Chat agent
Build a Next.js app I can deploy to Vercel called Heat Line. Main screen looks like a text-message thread on a phone. The tenant types an address and a message like "no heat, 61 inside". The agent replies in short, kind, plain English, says whether it's a violation and why, and never gives legal advice beyond stating the rule. Explain each step to me in plain English as you go.

Add a button "Draft HPD complaint + landlord letter" that asks Claude to write both using the timeline. Real texting probably won't happen tonight.US text messaging needs carrier registration that takes days, and Twilio trial accounts can only text pre-verified numbers. Use the chat screen that looks like texting, and say "SMS next week."
Address matching.311 stores addresses in capitals ("23 INDIA STREET"). Pick the demo building in advance and test it.
Sounding like a lawyer.Show the rule and the facts, not advice. Judges will respect that.
Neighbors are pretend in the demo.Say so on stage: "Simulated neighbors, real city data." Honesty scores better than a fake that gets caught.

Same for all three, before 6:30: one teammate has an Anthropic API key with a few dollars of credit, a Vercel account, and a RevenueCat account with a test web purchase link.
